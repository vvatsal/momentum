Permutation and Combination

Key formulas, problem types, solution methods, and one worked example for each major type.

1
Core ideas

What to check first

  • Permutation: use when order matters, such as arrangements, rankings, seating in a row.
  • Combination: use when order does not matter, such as teams, committees, selections.
  • Factorial: counts arrangements of all distinct objects.
  • Always identify whether repetition is allowed and whether there are restrictions.
n! = n(n-1)(n-2)... 1,   0! = 1
ⁿPᵣ = n! / (n-r)!
ⁿCᵣ = n! / [r!(n-r)!]
ⁿPᵣ = ⁿCᵣ × r!
2
Type 1

Selection vs arrangement

How to solve: Read the wording carefully.

  • Choose, select, form a team → combination.
  • Arrange, seat, rank, order → permutation.

Example: From 8 students, choose a team of 3.

Since order does not matter, use ⁸C₃ = 8!/(3!5!) = 56.

Answer: 56 teams.

Example: Arrange 4 different books on a shelf.

Order matters, so use ⁴P₄ = 4! = 24.

Answer: 24 arrangements.

3
Type 2

Arrange all objects

Distinct objects: If all n objects are different, number of arrangements = n!.

Example: Arrange the letters of MATH.

There are 4 distinct letters, so ways = 4! = 24.

Repeated objects: If some objects are identical, divide by factorials of repeated counts.

Arrangements = n! / (p!q!...)

Example: Arrange the letters of LEVEL.

5 letters with L repeated 2 times and E repeated 2 times.

Ways = 5!/(2!2!) = 120/4 = 30.

4
Type 3

Restricted arrangements

  • Together: treat the required group as one block.
  • Not together: total arrangements minus arrangements together.
  • For vowels/consonants restrictions, first create valid slots or blocks.

Example: A, B, C, D, E sit in a row, and A and B must sit together.

Treat AB as one block. Then we arrange AB, C, D, E in 4! ways.

Inside the block, A and B can switch in 2! ways.

Total = 4! × 2! = 24 × 2 = 48.

Answer: 48 ways.

5
Type 4

At least / at most conditions

How to solve:

  • Break into cases and add, or
  • Use complement: valid = total − unwanted.

Example: Form a committee of 4 from 6 men and 4 women with at least 1 woman.

Total committees = ¹⁰C₄ = 210.

Unwanted case: no woman, so choose all 4 from 6 men = ⁶C₄ = 15.

Required count = 210 − 15 = 195.

Answer: 195 committees.

6
Type 5

Distribution problems

Case A: Distinct objects to distinct persons or boxes.

Usually use the multiplication principle or permutations.

Example: 3 different prizes are given to 5 students, and a student may win more than one prize.

Each prize has 5 choices, so total = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125.

Case B: Identical objects to distinct boxes, zero allowed.

x₁ + x₂ + ... + xₙ = k

Number of non-negative solutions = (k+n−1)C(n−1).

Example: Distribute 5 identical balls into 3 boxes.

Ways = (5+3−1)C(3−1) = 7C₂ = 21.

7
Type 6 and 7

Repetition and circular arrangement

Permutation with repetition allowed: if each of r positions can be filled by any of n objects, number of ways = nʳ.

Example: 4-digit numbers from 0–9, repetition allowed, first digit not 0.

First digit: 9 choices. Remaining 3 digits: 10 choices each.

Total = 9 × 10³ = 9000.

Circular permutation: n distinct objects around a circle can be arranged in (n−1)! ways.

Example: 5 people around a circular table.

Ways = (5−1)! = 4! = 24.

Answer: 24 circular arrangements.
